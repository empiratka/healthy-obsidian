## Eating patterns and type 2 diabetes risk in men: breakfast omission, eating frequency, and snacking

type: [[study_design_cross-sectional|cross-sectional]]
## Population
- 29,206 US [[context_sex_male|men]]
- who were free of [[condition_diabetes_type2|T2DM]], [[condition_cardiovascular_disease|cardiovascular disease]] and [[condition_cancer|cancer]]
## Duration
- 16 years
## Findings
- After adjustment for known risk factors for [[condition_diabetes_type2|T2DM]], including [[metric_anthropometric_body_mass_index|BMI]], men who [[behavior_meal_distribution_breakfast_omission|skipped breakfast]] had 21% higher risk of [[condition_diabetes_type2|T2DM]] than did men who consumed breakfast.
- Compared with men who ate [[behavior_meal_frequency_medium|3 times/d]], men who ate [[behavior_meal_frequency_low|1–2 times/d]] had a higher risk of [[condition_diabetes_type2|T2DM]].
- These findings persisted after stratification by [[metric_anthropometric_body_mass_index|BMI]] or [[diet_quality|diet quality]].
- [[behavior_meal_frequency_high|Snacks beyond the 3 main meals]] were associated with increased [[condition_diabetes_type2|T2DM]] risk, but these associations were attenuated after adjustment for [[metric_anthropometric_body_mass_index|BMI]].
