## A Prospective Study of Breakfast Eating and Incident Coronary Heart Disease in a Cohort of Male U.S. Health Professionals

type: [[study]]
## Population
- 26 902 American [[context_sex_male|men]] 45 to 82 years
- who were free of cardiovascular disease and cancer
## Duration
- 16 years
## Findings
- Men who [[behavior_meal_distribution_breakfast_omission|skipped breakfast]] had a 27% higher risk of [[condition_coronary_heart_disease|CHD]] as compared with men who did not.
- Compared with men who did not eat late at night, those who [[behavior_meal_distribution_dinner_late|ate late]] at night had a 55% higher [[condition_coronary_heart_disease|CHD]] risk.
- These associations were mediated by [[metric_anthropometric_body_mass_index|BMI]], [[condition_hypertension|hypertension]], [[condition_hypercholesterolemia|hypercholesterolemia]] and [[condition_diabetes|diabetes]].
- No association was observed between [[behavior_meal_frequency|eating frequency]] (times/day) and risk of [[condition_coronary_heart_disease|CHD]]
