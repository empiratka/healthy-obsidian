Population:
- [[context_sex_female|women]]
- [[condition_obesity|Overweight / obese]]
- With [[metabolic_syndrome]]

Study type:
- Randomized controlled trial
- Isocaloric diets (~1400 kcal/day)
- Duration: 12 weeks

Intervention:
- High-calorie breakfast (700 kcal breakfast / 200 kcal dinner)
vs
- High-calorie dinner (200 kcal breakfast / 700 kcal dinner)

Key results:
- Greater weight loss and waist circumference reduction in high-calorie breakfast group
- Fasting glucose, insulin, and [[HOMA-IR]] decreased more significantly with high-calorie breakfast
- Triglycerides:
  - ↓ 33.6% in breakfast-loaded diet
  - ↑ 14.6% in dinner-loaded diet
- Oral glucose tolerance improved more in breakfast-loaded diet
- Lower daily glucose, insulin, ghrelin, and hunger scores
- Higher satiety scores throughout the day

Conclusion:
Distributing most daily calories to breakfast and reducing dinner intake improves weight loss, insulin sensitivity, lipid profile, and appetite regulation compared to calorie-matched diets with high-calorie dinners.

Limitations:
- Short duration (12 weeks)
- Specific population (women with metabolic syndrome)
- Long-term sustainability unknown
